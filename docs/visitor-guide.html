Geometry & Craft · Board III of III

Reading Aalto’s Signatures

One of only two buildings in the United States designed by Finnish master Alvar Aalto, Mount Angel unites severe rectilinear monastic masonry on the quad with a free, organic fan facing nature.

Four Shifted Compass Origins & Three-Tier Roof Hierarchy

SIGNATURE I · THRESHOLD & ACOUSTICS
Wood Screen & Twin Squiggle Walls

In the Lobby, 32 vertical wood battens veil the restroom doors in warm shadow. Steps away, a 28°-canted wood-fin screen and an S-curved plaster wall diffuse footsteps before they enter the Reading Well.

SIGNATURE II · TRANSPARENCY
14-Bay Birch Glass Wall & Ceiling Drum

Fourteen 40″ birch-mullioned clear glass bays sit atop a black-capped white wainscot, sharing borrowed north light with the Director’s Office and Periodical Lounge beside the radial slatted ceiling.

SIGNATURE III · PARAMETERIZED MILLWORK
Variable-Length Birch Bookcases

Almost every stack row has its own bespoke length (16.3′–20.0′) to preserve organic sightlines, crafted on ~3′-1″ bays with recessed black-linoleum kicks and 5-batten directory stanchions.

SIGNATURE IV · SCULPTURAL SOUND
Rare Book Auditorium (Rm 327)

Beneath its own taller +18′-8″ dark-membrane roof, a three-wave undulating plaster shell projects unamplified voice from the quarter-circle SE birch stage across fanned seating rows.
