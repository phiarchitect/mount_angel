Building the Forms with Code

In Aalto’s radial fan, almost no two walls, roof bays, or bookcases share the same length or angle. Rather than pushing vertices by hand, every solid in this model is generated from Python functions driven by Aalto’s survey dimensions.

When a blueprint re-read changed a roof elevation or column angle, the entire building regenerated cleanly in two seconds.

def add_aalto_bookcase_ft(mesh, center_ft, length_ft, depth_ft, angle_deg, ...):
    # Every radial stack in Aalto's fan has its own length (4 to 28 ft).
    # Subdivide into ~3'-1" bays (Sheet 13-a13 Details 9-12).
    n_bays = max(1, round(length_ft / 3.10))
    bay_span_ft = (length_ft - (n_bays + 1) * DIVIDER_THICK_FT) / n_bays

    # 1. Recessed linoleum kick plinth (Detail 10: 1.5" face inset)
    _add_box_local_ft(
        mesh, cx, cy, ux, uy, nx, ny,
        -half_l + 0.07, half_l - 0.07, -half_d + 0.125, half_d - 0.125,
        z_floor_m + TOL_M, z_shelf_bot - TOL_M, plinth_mat,
    )

    # 2. Solid 1" birch dividers + shelves + deterministic book clusters
    for i in range(n_bays + 1):
        s0 = -half_l + i * (bay_span_ft + DIVIDER_THICK_FT)
        _add_box_local_ft(
            mesh, cx, cy, ux, uy, nx, ny,
            s0, s0 + DIVIDER_THICK_FT, -half_d, half_d,
            z_shelf_bot, z_canopy_bot, birch_mat,
        )

02 · The IngeTrazo Feedback Loop

Write Code, Inspect in 3D, Check Topology, Repeat

Writing geometry in code is fast, but blind on its own: a wrong sign or wall offset can send a staircase straight through a brick facade. Coupling the Python builders with IngeTrazo and automated checks turned modeling into a tight visual loop.

STEP 01 · BUILD

Hot-Reload into IngeTrazo

build_ingetrazo_library.py reloads all modules over IngeTrazo’s live TCP/MCP bridge (--live), updating scene.groups in one undoable step and saving .igz + .obj.

STEP 02 · VERIFY

Zero-Leak Manifold Gate

A directed-edge check verifies unpaired == 0 on every solid. Any missing cap or reversed polygon is caught immediately before it can break stencil section cuts.

STEP 03 · CLEAN

Crease & Normal Filtering

On export to WebGL2, internal triangulation diagonals on flat walls and curved plaster vaults are stripped automatically so edges read like architectural ink lines.

STEP 04 · COMPARE

20 Locked Studio Cameras

Headless Chromium renders 20 fixed exterior, interior, section, and sun views after each pass—revealing collisions, shadow artifacts, and blueprint mismatches right away.

04 · Case Study · Seven Overlay Passes

Honing In on the Three H-Columns

At 700 pixels tall, the dimension text on Sheet 06-a6 around the three north perimeter piers (B, C, and D) was illegible. Rather than guessing, we projected Python test polygons onto the scan and refined the generator across seven passes—then carried the same loop into 3D.

05 · Caught in the Viewport

Four More Bugs Visual Inspection Exposed

Even when code runs cleanly and every mesh is watertight, architectural logic can still clash in 3D space. Here are four real issues spotted in viewport screenshots and fixed in Python.

Catch 01 · Diagonal Wall Math

Carrel Panels & Staircase Bleeding Through the East Flank

Early radial stack and stair placement ignored the inward thickness of the angled East brick wall (PT_E → PT_LOUNGE_JUNC), leaving two white carrel partitions and a stair stringer poking outside. Shifting both along the wall’s inward normal tucked them cleanly inside.

Catch 02 · Section Re-read

Inverted Roof Step Between the Lobby and the Fan

Our first pass stepped the South Wing roof up above a flat grey Fan roof. Checking Sheet 13-a13 Section A-A and aerial photos showed the opposite: the Fan’s 3′-11″ structural plenum steps +2′-7″ above the lower Lobby roof, while the SE Auditorium rises higher still.

Catch 03 · Coplanar Z-Fighting (v0.4.1)

Shadow Tearing Across the Auditorium Roof

Turning on 4096×4096 solar shadows revealed jagged brown triangles across the SE Auditorium roof. The closed-loop brick wall builder had capped its own interior footprint at z = +5.69 m—the exact elevation of the dark roof membrane. Dropping internal wall caps below the slab cured it.

Catch 04 · Detail Sheet Check (v0.4.2)

Continuing the Step Parapet Behind the Daylight Scoop

Looking down from the South roof showed the copper-coped brick step parapet stopping abruptly at the two ends of the curved skylight. Sheet 18-a18 Detail 4 showed the parapet continues unbroken along the 24′-0″ arc right behind the scoop’s plaster springing.
