Fr. Barnabas Reasoner, O.S.B. · March 8–18, 1966

The Journey to Helsinki

After three years of delays, Father Abbot sent the Abbey’s librarian to Finland to meet Alvar Aalto and move the library forward. In ten days he saw Aalto’s studio and buildings, met the architect in Zurich, and came home with a clear picture of what the library would be.

Click a stop to read about it. His exact flight route isn’t recorded; the lines show the order of the visits.

Before the trip

Berkeley

He first met Vernon DeMars and Wells, Aalto’s American associates. DeMars had shared an office with Aalto at M.I.T., and gave practical advice: Aalto reacts to people and personal contact.

So Father Abbot agreed he should go to Helsinki in person.

March 8

Portland

He left from Portland Airport. The whole round trip cost slightly less than $1,000.

March 9 · 11:30 pm

Helsinki

Aalto sent his own car and chauffeur to the airport, and Eric Vartiainen, who would later supervise the library’s construction in Oregon, met him. He stayed as the guest of Aalto’s office, where the staff spoke proudly of their Oregon project.

In the studio he photographed a model of the two seminaries and the library, and saw the latest plans. With Kaarlo Leppänen and Eric he worked through the site: the quiet north side, the road, and how the building should meet the hill.

He also visited Aalto’s newest Helsinki buildings: the concert hall (the House of Culture) and the Technical Institute campus at Otaniemi, whose library was then being built.

Photographs are present-day or period images from Wikimedia Commons, not Fr. Barnabas’s own.

“It is a cheerful experience to enter an Aalto building on a gloomy day… a feeling of coming-into-the-light not a going out of it.” Fr. Barnabas Reasoner, Helsinki Report
On the Arctic Circle

Rovaniemi

He travelled north to Aalto’s most recently finished building, the library at Rovaniemi, larger than the one planned for Mount Angel. He took photographs and colour transparencies to show the community.

His verdict: The building is magnificent in its interiors. Its sunken, fan-shaped reading room under skylights is a close cousin of ours.

Mentioned

Seinäjoki

He also noted Aalto’s library at Seinäjoki, completed in 1965. Aalto had stopped taking new library commissions so that each one would be organically worked out to suit its own peculiar needs.

The meeting

Zurich

Aalto, aged 68 and tired, was resting in Switzerland on his doctor’s orders. He came into the city to meet Fr. Barnabas. Libraries are his pets, he was told; Aalto said that while other buildings change, the function and place of a library remains constant.

Of the site there had never been any doubt: ‘It belongs there’ and he made a spreading gesture with his hand. Over dinner he repeated a friend’s remark that the only true gentlemen in America were the Benedictines.

March 18

Home via Brussels

Sabena routed him home through Zurich at no extra cost. Back at the Abbey he wrote his report, promising the photographs for the Fathers’ Recreation Room.

What he hoped for

“A person entering the library will (on a clear day) see… the valley below and the Cascade Range crowned with Mt. Hood.”On the picture window
“…a pleasant controlled space with an emphasis on quiet and disengagement.”On the reading room
“The outside was because of the inside. It is the interior beauty of the space… which gives form to the outside.”Aalto’s office, as he reported it

Help us find his photographs

Fr. Barnabas photographed Aalto’s model of the Mount Angel buildings, the Rovaniemi library and several Helsinki buildings, including colour transparencies. We haven’t found them yet. If you know where they might be, please let the library know.
